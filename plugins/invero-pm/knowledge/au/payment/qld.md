# Security of Payment — Queensland

The governing Act is the **Building Industry Fairness (Security of Payment) Act 2017 (Qld)**.

**This file is Queensland only.** It is one jurisdiction of eight and carries no authority
anywhere else — `README.md` in this folder has the shape common to all of them. **Victorian day counts, and the post-15-April-2026 Victorian regime — abolished reference dates, capped payment terms, the s 13A unfair-time-bar test — do not travel to Queensland.** Queensland runs a different Act on a different structure, described below; nothing in the Victorian file answers a Queensland question, and
this file's own periods are not written here either, for the reason the folder rule gives.

## Two chapters, two questions — read the right one

The Act is not only a progress-payments Act. **Chapter 2 is a statutory trust regime**: its
purpose, stated at s 7, is to ensure funds paid to a contracted party under particular contracts
are held on trust to protect subcontractors' interests — project trusts and retention trusts,
administered by the QBCC. **Chapter 3 is the progress-payments machinery** — payment claims,
payment schedules and adjudication — and is what this file describes. A project manager asking
"has this claim been served correctly" is a Chapter 3 question; a project manager asking "is the
principal required to hold claimed retention on trust" is a Chapter 2 question, and this file
does not answer it. Establish which chapter the question belongs to before reading further, and
if the answer is Chapter 2, treat that as its own gap rather than reasoning from this page.

## The mechanism (Chapter 3)

1. A person who carries out construction work or supplies related goods and services has a
   statutory right to a **progress payment** from each **reference date** — s 70, with reference
   date defined at s 67.
2. The claimant serves a **payment claim** — s 68 defines it, s 75 governs making it.
3. The respondent serves a **payment schedule** in response, stating what it proposes to pay and,
   if less than claimed, why — s 69 defines it, s 76 governs the response.
4. If no schedule is served, the claimant is entitled to be paid the full claimed amount — s 77.
   If a schedule is served but not paid, or no schedule is served and payment does not follow, the
   claimant may recover the amount as a debt or apply for **adjudication** — s 78, with Part 4
   (ss 79–97) governing adjudication.

## Queensland retains the reference date — Victoria has abolished it

**This is the single largest trap for anyone trained on the post-15-April-2026 Victorian
position.** Victoria abolished reference dates; Queensland has not. Under s 67, a reference date
is either the date the contract fixes (or a method the contract fixes) or, failing that, the
last day of each month in which work was carried out — and s 67(2) fixes the final reference date
on termination where the contract is silent or purports to prevent one surviving termination. The
right to a progress payment runs from each reference date (s 70), and a claimant cannot make more
than one payment claim per reference date (s 75(4)). Identifying the correct reference date is
therefore the first step in Queensland, not a formality — get it wrong and the claim itself is
open to challenge on validity, independently of anything to do with the amount.

## Where each clock lives in the Act

No periods are written here — the folder rule. This list exists so a reader goes to the section
that fixes a period rather than to another jurisdiction's number, or to a number that has drifted.

- **When a payment claim may be served — s 75(2) and (3).** A **final payment** claim runs on a
  different measure from every other claim — s 75(3) ties it to the end of the defects liability
  period and to completion, not to the reference-date rhythm that governs every claim before it.
  Confirm which regime a claim falls under before counting anything.
- **The payment schedule period — s 76(1).** The contract may fix a period; the Act fixes a
  maximum; s 76(1) runs on **whichever ends first**. As in New South Wales, a contract period can
  only bring the date forward, never push it out.
- **The due date for payment — s 73(1).** The contract governs if it provides for the matter;
  failing that, s 73(1)(b) fixes the default, running from when the payment claim was made. Note
  s 73's own cross-references to the **Queensland Building and Construction Commission Act 1991**
  — see below.
- **Consequence of no schedule — s 77.** The claimant becomes entitled to the full claimed amount
  on the due date; no further step is needed to establish the entitlement, only to enforce it.
- **Reasons that may be run at adjudication — s 82(3)–(4), read against s 69(c) and s 88(2)–(3).**
  See the section below; this is the second-largest trap in the Act.
- **The adjudication application window — s 79(2)(b).** The Act sets out **three different
  triggering events with three different periods** — a claim met with no schedule at all, a
  claim met with a schedule whose amount goes unpaid, and a claim scheduled for less than claimed
  — sub-subparagraphs (i), (ii) and (iii). Establish which of the three applies before counting;
  they are not interchangeable and the period runs from a different day in each case.
- **The adjudication response window, and the decision window — s 83 and s 85.** Both distinguish
  **standard** from **complex** payment claims (see below) and run different periods for each.
- **What the adjudicator may and must not consider — s 88(2) and (3).** See below.
- **Suspension of work for non-payment — s 98**, following notice given under s 78(3)/(4) or
  s 92(1)/(2). A minimum notice period applies before the right to suspend crystallises; establish
  it from s 98 itself, not from a Victorian or NSW suspension habit.

## Standard and complex payment claims are not the same claim

Queensland is the only East Coast Act among the ones written into this folder so far that
**splits the claim itself into two classes with different machinery.** Section 64 defines a
**complex payment claim** by reference to a claimed-amount threshold (with the Act allowing the
threshold to be varied by regulation — treat the current figure as something to confirm at the
Act itself, the same discipline this file applies to periods, rather than as a fixed number
written here). Everything that is not complex is a **standard payment claim**. The distinction
matters because **s 83 (adjudication response time) and s 85 (decision time) run different
periods for each class**, and s 83(3)–(4) allows an extension of time specific to a complex claim
that a standard claim cannot access. Establish the class before diarising either clock.

## Reasons not in the schedule cannot be run at adjudication

**The same discipline as every other jurisdiction in this folder, from a different set of
sections.** A payment schedule must state its reasons if it proposes to pay less than claimed
(s 69(c)). An adjudication response must not include a reason for withholding payment that was
not already in the payment schedule (s 82(4)) — the Act calls this a "new reason" — and the
adjudicator may require the response to be resubmitted without it (s 82(5)). Independently, the
adjudicator is told exactly what it may consider (s 88(2): the Act, the contract, the claim and
its submissions, the schedule and its submissions, and the adjudicator's own inspection) and told
in the same section what it **must not** consider — a late adjudication response, or a reason
prohibited under s 82 (s 88(3)). The schedule's face is therefore the whole record, in Queensland
exactly as it is everywhere else this folder describes: state every reason, on the schedule, at
the time it is served.

## The supporting statement — a Queensland-specific trap with no Victorian or NSW equivalent

Where a claim is made under a subcontract and the construction contract itself is not also a
subcontract for a further contract, s 75(6)–(7) requires the claimant to **accompany the payment
claim with a supporting statement** — a declaration that all subcontractors have been paid, or a
schedule of who has not been and why (s 75(9) defines its content). Failing to provide one is an
offence (s 75(7), maximum penalty stated in the Act) but — critically — **does not invalidate the
payment claim itself** (s 75(8)). Nothing else in this folder's other jurisdiction files carries
an equivalent requirement; do not assume it and do not assume its absence elsewhere.

## The Queensland Building and Construction Commission Act 1991 sits alongside this Act

Section 73's own notes point outward: a provision in a construction management trade contract or
subcontract that sets a progress-payment period longer than the cap the QBCC Act fixes is void
under that Act's s 67U, and the equivalent cap for a commercial building contract is in QBCC Act
s 67W; the penalty interest rate that applies to a building contract under this Act's s 73(2)-(3)
is set by QBCC Act s 67P. The regulator for both Acts is the **Queensland Building and
Construction Commission (QBCC)**, and a failure to give a payment schedule is separately grounds
for QBCC disciplinary action (note to s 76(1)). A Queensland project manager cannot read the BIF
Act's progress-payment chapter in isolation from the QBCC Act's own period caps and penalty
regime — the two are cross-referenced by the BIF Act itself, not merely adjacent.

## Why a PM must care

**The reasons for withholding must be on the face of the payment schedule.** A respondent who
fails to serve a schedule in time is liable for the full claimed amount under s 77 with nothing
further to prove; one who serves a schedule that omits a reason cannot introduce it later under
s 82(4). This is the same trap the rest of this folder describes: a client-side PM who lets an
informal dispute stand in for a compliant schedule loses the argument on the calendar, not on
the merits.

**The dates are hard deadlines, not targets**, and in Queensland there are more of them running
at once than in Victoria or New South Wales — the reference date, the schedule date, the payment
due date, and, once a dispute is live, three different adjudication-application triggers and two
different response/decision tracks depending on claim size. Establish the reference date and the
claim class first; every other date in the chapter is measured from one or the other.

## Practical rules

- **Fix the reference date before doing anything else.** Everything — the claim's validity, the
  one-claim-per-reference-date rule at s 75(4), the final-payment exception at s 75(3) — depends
  on getting this right first.
- **Establish the claim class — standard or complex — under s 64** before diarising the
  adjudication response or decision clock; they are not the same length and the extension
  mechanism at s 83(3)–(4) only exists for one of them.
- **State every reason on the schedule.** Not the main one. Every one. s 82(4) bars anything else
  at adjudication, and s 88(3) tells the adjudicator not to consider it even if it is submitted.
- **Check whether a supporting statement is required** under s 75(6)–(7) before treating a
  subcontract-tier claim as complete — its absence is an offence even though it does not
  invalidate the claim.
- **Do not assume the QBCC Act is a separate question.** Confirm the applicable period caps
  (ss 67U, 67W) and penalty interest rate (s 67P) for the specific contract type at project
  establishment, alongside the BIF Act itself.
- **Do not reason from Victoria or New South Wales.** Victoria has abolished reference dates and
  capped payment terms under a 2025 amending Act with retrospective effect; none of that applies
  here. New South Wales requires claim endorsement under its s 13(2)(c) — nothing of that kind
  was found in the sections of the Queensland Act read for this file (ss 60–98); do not assume
  Queensland shares it, and do not assume it does not, on the strength of an absence in this
  reading alone.

## Where the project manager sits

The project manager assesses and drafts; **the Principal serves.** Nothing in this file changes that, and no agent serves a payment claim, payment schedule or adjudication document on its own initiative.

## Contracts can shorten, never lengthen

As in every jurisdiction this folder covers, a contract may set a shorter period than the Act's
maximum where the Act allows it to (s 76(1) is explicit that the earlier of the two governs); it
cannot lengthen one the Act fixes outright, such as the three adjudication-application windows at
s 79(2)(b). Establish both the contract's periods and the Act's at project setup and record them in the contract calendar and in the project's `project_facts`, never in this file.
