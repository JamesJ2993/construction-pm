# Which NCC edition governs a job

**The edition current when the approval was issued — not the newest one published.**

This is the single most common way a compliance check goes wrong. A job approved under NCC 2019 is
still assessed under NCC 2019, however many editions have landed since. The agent records
`ncc_edition` per project in the registry for exactly this reason.

## Editions

| Edition | Status as at 21.08.2026 |
|---|---|
| NCC 2016 | Superseded |
| NCC 2019 (+ Amendment 1) | Superseded, but still governs jobs approved under it |
| NCC 2022 | Superseded by 2025, still governs jobs approved under it |
| **NCC 2025** | **Current.** Preview released 01.02.2026; jurisdictions may adopt from 01.05.2026. |

Adoption is a state and territory decision, so the date NCC 2025 actually applies varies by
jurisdiction, and transition arrangements usually let a job already in train continue under the
previous edition. **Check the adoption position for the job's state before citing a clause.**

## What changed in NCC 2025 that matters to retail fitout

Detail in [retail-fitout-hotspots.md](retail-fitout-hotspots.md). In summary:

- **Section J energy efficiency** — the largest set of changes. Lighting control moved from motion
  detectors to demand-operated controls (J7D3–D4), on-site solar photovoltaic mandated (J9D5), energy
  monitoring required (J9D3), HVAC efficiency tightened, and several climate zone concessions removed.
- **Sanitary facilities** — all-gender facilities permitted as an alternative (F4D4), and sanitary
  product disposal and dispensers now required (F4D6, F4D8).
- **Wayfinding signage** — new requirement (D1P10, D3D31).
- **Carpark fire safety** — open-deck carpark concessions removed unless adequately sprinkler
  protected (E1D2–E1D9, S5C9 and following).
- **Water management** — Part F1 restructured and Part F3 deleted into it.
- **Condensation** — membrane vapour permeance specified, separate provisions for parallel and
  non-parallel roof insulation (F8D3, F8D5–D6).

## The trap

A tenancy fitout inside a base building approved under an earlier edition can find itself
straddling two editions: the base building assessed under one, the fitout approval under another.
That is normal, but it means a base building condition and a fitout requirement can be written
against different rules. When they appear to conflict, check which edition each was assessed under
before raising it as a design conflict — it may be a transition artefact rather than an error.
