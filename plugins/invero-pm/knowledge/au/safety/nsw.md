# NSW Codes of Practice — the status changed on 1 July 2026

> **Where the project manager sits.** Written for a consultant PM who performs no construction work, engages no subcontractors and does not direct the head contractor's means, methods or safety systems. This file exists so that a PM knows what binds the parties who *do*, and can observe and report against it. Nothing here makes the PM a duty holder for the head contractor's obligations, and no finding
> drawn from it may be worded as a direction, an approval or a certification.

**This is the most consequential recent WHS change and it is easy to miss.**

Under **section 26A of the Work Health and Safety Act 2011 (NSW)**, from **1 July 2026** approved
Codes of Practice became the minimum performance standard a PCBU is expected to comply with. A
business must either follow the applicable Code, or manage the hazard in a way that is **equivalent
or higher** than the standard the Code sets.

Before that date they were guidance. They are not guidance any more.

Two further consequences worth knowing:

- Inspectors may reference a Code when issuing a notice.
- A court may treat a Code as evidence of what is known about a hazard, a risk, a risk assessment or
  a risk control.

## What this changes for a PM

- **Subcontractor SWMS need to align to the applicable Code, not merely to "safe practice".** A SWMS
  that does not address what the Code addresses is now a weaker document than it used to be.
- **"We manage it differently" is still available, but it now has to be demonstrated.** That means a
  documented risk assessment showing the alternative is equal or better — not an assertion.
- Where a head contractor or principal contractor imposes its own safety system, check it is at
  least at Code standard for the relevant hazards. Inheriting someone else's system does not
  transfer the duty.

## Tunnels and shafts — a new code, not one a retail fitout normally engages

**New approved code: "Tunnels and shafts in construction" (August 2026).** Confirmed present on the
SafeWork NSW approved-codes list on 10 September 2026. The pre-WHS-Act code "Tunnels under
construction" — a legacy instrument that predated the 2011 Act — no longer appears on the current
list and is treated as retired.

This does not touch a typical fitout. It is recorded here so the
approved-codes table above stays a complete list, and so the fact is on hand the day a NSW
civil-adjacent commission — basement excavation with shaft access, services tunnelling — lands. It
is deliberately not added to the "Codes relevant to construction and fitout" table above, because
that table is scoped to what a fitout normally engages and this code is not that.

## Respirable crystalline silica

The RCS Code is new (February 2026) and lands directly on shopfitting: engineered stone benchtops,
cutting or drilling concrete and masonry, and tile cutting. Given the mandatory status from 1 July
2026, an RCS control that was previously handled informally now needs to meet the Code or be shown to
be equal or better.

## Asbestos

Two separate Codes — managing and controlling it in the workplace, and safely removing it. A tenancy
in an older building is the classic exposure: the base building asbestos register governs, and the
fitout must not disturb what the register identifies. **Ask for the register before any demolition or
penetration work**, not after.

## What to verify per project

- Which Codes the specific scope engages
- Whether the principal contractor's safety system meets or exceeds them
- Whether SWMS have been updated since 1 July 2026, or still read as pre-mandatory documents
