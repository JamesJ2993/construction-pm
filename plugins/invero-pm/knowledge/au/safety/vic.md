# Victoria — occupational health and safety

**Victoria is the only Australian jurisdiction that never adopted the model WHS laws.** It runs
the **Occupational Health and Safety Act 2004 (Vic)** and the Occupational Health and Safety
Regulations, administered by WorkSafe Victoria. See `README.md` in this branch for the
jurisdiction table.

> **Where the project manager sits.** Written for a consultant PM who performs no construction work, engages no subcontractors and does not direct the head contractor's means, methods or safety systems. This file exists so a project manager knows what binds the parties who do, and can observe and report against it. Nothing here makes the PM a duty holder for those obligations, and no
> finding drawn from it may be worded as a direction, an approval or a certification.

## What actually differs

The instinct to treat Victoria as "NSW with different letterhead" is the trap. Three practical
differences:

- **The vocabulary is different.** Victoria says *occupational health and safety*, not *work
  health and safety*. Its duty holder language, its notification categories and its terminology
  for the persons who hold duties do not map one-to-one onto the model Act's *PCBU*. Using model
  WHS vocabulary in a Victorian document reads as unfamiliarity with the jurisdiction.
- **The instruments are different.** Victoria issues **compliance codes** and compliance code
  guidance notes, not *approved codes of practice*. A NSW approved code of practice has no
  application in Victoria, and the s 26A status change described in `nsw.md` is a NSW provision
  with no Victorian equivalent.
- **The duty structure is its own.** The OHS Act 2004 predates the model laws and was not
  rewritten to match them. Do not reason from a model Act section number to a Victorian one.

## What this means for a project manager

- **Establish the jurisdiction at project setup**, and record it in the project's
  `project_facts`. A national portfolio running both regimes at once will otherwise answer a Victorian question from the NSW file, which is the failure
  this branch exists to prevent.
- **Observe against the right instrument.** A site observation report referencing a NSW approved
  code on a Victorian site is wrong on its face and undermines everything else in the report.
  The site-report agent governs how observations are written; this file governs what they are observed against.
- **The head contractor holds the duties.** The PM reports what it observed, where, on what date
  and against which drawing revision. It does not assess the adequacy of a safety system, and
  does not direct a change to one.
