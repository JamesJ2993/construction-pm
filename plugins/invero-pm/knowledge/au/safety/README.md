# Work health and safety — which statute applies where

**Read this before opening any jurisdiction file.** The most common WHS mistake in a national
practice is assuming the law is the same everywhere. It is not, and the exception is Victoria.

## Where the project manager sits

This pack is written for a **consultant project manager acting for the client** who performs no construction work, engages no subcontractors and does not direct the head contractor's means, methods or safety systems. If the firm using it holds a different role (recorded as `project_facts.role`), read the duty-holder material accordingly. Nothing in this branch makes the PM a duty holder for the head contractor's obligations. It exists so a project manager
knows what binds the parties who *do* carry out the work, and can observe and report against it.
No finding drawn from these files may be worded as a direction, an approval or a certification.

## The model laws, and the one jurisdiction that never adopted them

| Jurisdiction | Principal statute | Model laws |
|---|---|---|
| **Victoria** | **Occupational Health and Safety Act 2004 (Vic)** and the OHS Regulations | **No — never adopted** |
| New South Wales | Work Health and Safety Act 2011 (NSW) | Yes, from 01.01.2012 |
| Queensland | Work Health and Safety Act 2011 (Qld) | Yes, from 01.01.2012 |
| Northern Territory | Work Health and Safety (National Uniform Legislation) Act 2011 (NT) | Yes, from 01.01.2012 |
| Australian Capital Territory | Work Health and Safety Act 2011 (ACT) | Yes, from 01.01.2012 |
| Commonwealth | Work Health and Safety Act 2011 (Cth) | Yes, from 01.01.2012 |
| South Australia | Work Health and Safety Act 2012 (SA) | Yes, from 01.01.2013 |
| Tasmania | Work Health and Safety Act 2012 (Tas) | Yes, from 01.01.2013 |
| Western Australia | Work Health and Safety Act 2020 (WA) | Yes — passed 10.11.2020, **operational 31.03.2022** |

**Victoria is the only jurisdiction that has not implemented the model WHS laws.** It runs its
own framework under the OHS Act 2004. The vocabulary differs, the duty structure differs, and
the instruments differ — Victoria has **compliance codes**, not approved codes of practice.

Two consequences that catch people out:

- **A NSW code of practice has no application to a Victorian site.** `nsw.md` does not transfer. A portfolio with jobs in both states runs both regimes at once.
- **Western Australia adopted late and adopted its own version.** Its regulations and codes are
  not interchangeable with the eastern states', even though the Act tracks the model.

Every model-law jurisdiction is also "expected to make variations to ensure the laws operate
effectively in their jurisdiction". Adoption of the model laws means the framework matches, not
that the detail does. Check the jurisdiction file, not the model.

## The status change that matters most

In the model-law jurisdictions, **approved codes of practice became mandatory rather than
advisory** — in NSW under WHS Act s 26A from 01.07.2026. A new approved code is therefore a new
obligation, not new guidance, and the currency scan treats it that way. See `nsw.md`.
