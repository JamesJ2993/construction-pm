# Fire services

## What the consultant issues

- Sprinkler layout, coordinated with the reflected ceiling plan
- Detection and alarm layout
- Hydrant and hose reel coverage where tenancy works affect them
- Passive fire schedule — penetration seals and fire-rated construction
- A fire engineering report where anything relies on a Performance Solution
- Commissioning certificates, and input to the fire safety measures schedule at handover

Sheet prefix is normally **F** or **FS**. Standards: AS 2118.1 sprinklers, AS 1670.1 detection,
AS 2419.1 hydrants, AS 2441 hose reels, AS 1851 for the maintenance regime after handover.

## Where it bites on a retail fitout

**Every ceiling change is a fire services change.** Move a ceiling, add a bulkhead, install a tall
joinery unit, and sprinkler heads and detectors move with it. Obstruction rules under AS 2118.1 are
the ones most often missed at tender, because the obstruction is joinery drawn on a different sheet
by a different consultant.

**Penetrations need a tested system, not a product.** A penetration seal must have a tested system
matching the actual substrate, the actual service and the actual FRL. A generic product datasheet is
not evidence. NCC 2025 tightened this further: fire hazard property and EW classification testing
must now be by an **Accredited Testing Laboratory** (C1V3, S7C4). Check the certificate names one.

**Fire hazard properties of linings.** Wall and ceiling linings, floor coverings and decorative
finishes all have indices under Section C. A material substitution during construction that looks
like a finish change can be a compliance change.

**Performance Solutions.** Fire is where they cluster — travel distance, smoke hazard management,
fire compartmentation. Any Performance Solution needs a report, a stated assessment method, and the
certifier's acceptance, and it flows into the fire safety schedule and the annual statement. **An
undocumented Performance Solution found late is an occupancy risk, not a drafting problem.**

**Fire safety measures schedule.** At handover the schedule lists every fire safety measure and the
standard it is maintained to. If the fitout added, removed or modified a measure, the schedule
changes. This is a common gap between "practical completion" and "actually able to occupy".

## Review checklist

- Does the sprinkler layout match the current reflected ceiling plan revision, or an older one?
- Are obstruction clearances shown for tall joinery, bulkheads and feature ceilings?
- Are detector positions clear of bulkheads and high units?
- Is every penetration of a fire-rated element on the passive fire schedule, with a tested system
  reference — not a product name?
- Are fire hazard property certificates provided for linings and finishes, and do they name an
  Accredited Testing Laboratory?
- Is anything relying on a Performance Solution? If so, is the report on the set, and has the
  certifier accepted it?
- Are hose reels and hydrants still accessible after the joinery is installed?
- Does the fire safety measures schedule reflect what the fitout actually changed?
- Is exit and directional signage coordinated with the electrical set — the two are often drawn
  separately and disagree?

## Common coordination clashes

| Clash | How it shows up |
|---|---|
| Sprinkler versus joinery | Tall shelving inside the obstruction envelope of a head |
| Sprinkler versus duct | Main and duct on the same axis at high level |
| Detector versus bulkhead | Detector on the wrong side of a new bulkhead, or inside a ceiling pocket |
| Hose reel versus stock | Reel behind a fixture, losing its coverage |
| Penetration seal versus substrate | Tested system specified for plasterboard, installed in blockwork |
| Exit sign versus ceiling | Sign position on E set conflicts with the RCP or a bulkhead |

## A note on register entries

Fire findings are the ones most likely to be written vaguely. "Sprinkler coordination required" is
not actionable. "Head at grid C3 sits 180mm from the face of joinery unit J-14, inside the AS 2118.1
obstruction envelope" is. Give the location, the measurement and the rule.

And a markup removed is not a conflict resolved — if a previous revision carried a cloud that has
simply disappeared, the question is whether the issue was fixed or the cloud was.

## Interface with the tenancy

Base building provides the sprinkler and detection infrastructure and the fire indicator panel; the
tenant modifies the tenancy-side distribution. Any change to the tenancy affects a base building
system, so **the landlord's fire contractor is usually mandated** — check the lease, because a
mandated contractor removes competitive pricing from that trade and needs to be priced accordingly.
