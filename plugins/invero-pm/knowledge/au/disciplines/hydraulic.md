# Hydraulic

## What the consultant issues

- Water service layout — cold, heated, and where relevant tempered
- Sanitary drainage layout with invert levels and falls
- Trade waste layout where there is a food offer
- Fixture schedule
- Backflow prevention details
- Plumbing certificate of compliance at completion

Sheet prefix is normally **H** or **P**. Governed by NCC Volume Three (the PCA) and the AS/NZS 3500
series, not Volume One.

## Where it bites on a retail fitout

**Falls to the existing stack.** This is the constraint that shapes everything. A new WC or sink has
to drain to a stack that already exists at a fixed invert. If the fall does not work, the options are
a step up in the floor, a pump, or moving the fixture — all three are design changes, and all three
are expensive if discovered after the layout is signed off.

**Ask for the invert level early.** A hydraulic set that shows a fixture without a drainage route and
an invert is not coordinated, it is aspirational.

**Backflow prevention.** The item most often left out of a tenancy scope. Any connection to the
landlord water service needs the right containment device for the hazard rating, and it needs annual
testing after handover. It appears on nobody's tender unless the PM puts it there.

**Trade waste.** Any food offer needs a trade waste application to the water authority, a grease
arrestor sized to the fixture load, and space for it. The approval has its own lead time, entirely
independent of the building approval, and it is a common cause of a delayed opening.

**Hot water.** Point-of-use versus central, and tempering for accessible fixtures. Tempering valves
need access for servicing, which the joinery often forgets.

**Waterproofing.** Not strictly hydraulic, but it fails at the hydraulic interface — penetrations
through a membrane. Waterproofing is an inspection hold point and needs its certificate at handover.
NCC 2025 restructured Part F1 and **deleted Part F3 into it**, so a citation to F3 is now stale.

## Review checklist

- Is every fixture shown with a drainage route, not just a symbol?
- Are invert levels stated at the connection to the existing stack?
- Does the fall work over the run shown? Check it, do not assume it.
- Is backflow prevention shown, with the device type and hazard rating?
- Is there a trade waste strategy where there is a food offer, including arrestor size and location?
- Do floor wastes coordinate with the structural penetration setout and the floor finish falls?
- Is tempered water provided to accessible fixtures, and is the valve accessible for service?
- Are wet areas identified, and does the waterproofing extent appear on the architectural set?
- Is the water meter or check meter arrangement shown, and does it match the lease?

## Common coordination clashes

| Clash | How it shows up |
|---|---|
| Floor waste versus tendon | Drainage penetration where the structural setout shows a post-tensioning band |
| Fall versus slab thickness | Required fall would take the drain below the slab soffit |
| Fixture versus joinery | Basin centreline on the hydraulic plan does not match the joinery elevation |
| Grease arrestor versus space | Arrestor sized correctly but with nowhere to put it that allows extraction for pump-out |
| Condensate versus drainage | Mechanical condensate with no compliant discharge point shown |

## Interface with the tenancy

The landlord provides a capped water service and a drainage connection point; everything downstream
is tenant work. The lease will name the connection points, and the base building drawings may or may
not show them accurately. **Verify on site before tender.** A capped point 8m from where the drawing
shows it is a variation nobody has priced.
