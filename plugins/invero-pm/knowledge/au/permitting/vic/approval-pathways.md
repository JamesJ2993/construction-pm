# Victoria — planning and building approval pathways

**This file is Victoria only.** Victoria runs its own planning and building statutes, its own
instruments and its own vocabulary, and **`nsw/approval-pathways.md` does not transfer to a
Victorian job** — there is no exempt development, no complying development certificate and no
private certifier issuing the planning approval. Reading the NSW file for a Victorian tenancy
produces a confident answer to a question it was not written about. See `README.md` in this
branch for which instrument governs which jurisdiction.

## The spine: two instruments, two authorities

Almost every avoidable programme failure on a Victorian fitout comes from treating these as one
approval.

| | Planning permit | Building permit |
|---|---|---|
| **Governing law** | *Planning and Environment Act 1987* (Vic) — the PE Act | *Building Act 1993* (Vic) and the *Building Regulations 2018* |
| **Who decides** | The **responsible authority** — in most cases the council | The **relevant building surveyor** — municipal, or a private building surveyor appointed under Part 6 of the Act |
| **Decided against** | The planning scheme for the municipality — zones, overlays, particular provisions, policy | The Act, the regulations and any binding determination |
| **What it controls** | Whether a use or a development may happen on that land at all | How the building work is carried out and whether it may be occupied |
| **Needed when** | Only where the scheme requires it for that use or development | For building work generally, whether or not a planning permit is required |

The two are **sequenced, not parallel**. Under s 24(1)(c) and (d) of the Building Act the relevant
building surveyor must not issue a building permit unless satisfied that any relevant planning
permit has been obtained and that the building permit will be consistent with it. A planning
permit in the wrong shape therefore stops the building permit, and a building permit cannot be
used to outrun a planning decision.

> **A person determines the pathway position on any specific job**, under the firm's legal-requirements procedure where it has one. This file is a triage tool: it tells a project manager what to ask and which authority
> to ask. It does not decide whether a permit is required for a particular tenancy, and no
> statement here is a legal conclusion about a site. The determination is made by a person, with the responsible authority and the relevant building surveyor.

## The planning permit

A planning permit is the legal document allowing a specified use or development on a specified
parcel of land. The benefit generally attaches to the land; it carries conditions and endorsed
plans that the proposal must satisfy, and it expires.

**The scheme is the test, and the scheme is local.** Each municipality has its own planning
scheme, built from the statewide **Victoria Planning Provisions**. Three parts of it decide
whether a permit is needed:

- **The zone.** All land is zoned. Each zone carries a table of uses in three sections —
  section 1 uses that need no planning permit, section 2 uses that need one, section 3 uses that
  are prohibited. A use in section 1 or 2 may carry conditions; fail the condition and the use
  can move up a section or out of the zone altogether. A use not named falls to "any other use",
  usually in section 2.
- **Overlays.** Not all land has one; some land has several. An overlay flags a feature —
  heritage, vegetation, flooding — and adds requirements to the zone rather than replacing them.
  Most overlays control development rather than use.
- **Particular provisions.** Statewide provisions for specified categories of use and
  development — **signs and car parking among them** — applying in addition to the zone and the
  overlay unless stated otherwise.

**Where these are read.** The **Planning Property Report** (planning.vic.gov.au) returns the zone
map, the overlay maps, links to the zone and overlay provisions in the local scheme, whether the
property is on the Victorian Heritage Register, whether it is in a designated bushfire prone area
and whether it is in an area of Aboriginal cultural heritage sensitivity. **VicPlan** is the
interactive statewide map behind it. The scheme ordinance itself is read at
**planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au**. Establish the zone and overlays from these before
anyone builds a programme.

### What to check on a fitout — checks, not conclusions

None of the following is a statement that a permit *is* required. Each is a question to put to
the scheme and, where the answer is not obvious on its face, to the council before lodgement:

- **Is the proposed use as-of-right in the zone?** A change of use is the trigger most often
  missed, because the fitout drawings look the same either way. The zone's table of uses decides
  it, and a section 1 use that fails its condition is not as-of-right.
- **Signage.** Signs are a particular provision, and the zone and any overlay bear on it. A
  shopfront sign is a common late permit trigger.
- **Car parking.** Parking is a particular provision; a reduction in the parking otherwise
  required is the kind of thing that turns an application into a contested one.
- **Heritage Overlay.** If one applies, works that would otherwise be invisible to the scheme —
  including demolition — can require a permit, and the council must weigh the effect on the
  significance of the place.
- **External works.** Anything crossing the lease line outward — shopfront, plant, awning,
  services penetrations — is where a permit obligation commonly appears.
- **Liquor.** A licensed use is both a scheme question and a separate licensing question. They
  are different approvals with different regulators; see below.

### VicSmart — the fast-track class

**VicSmart is a different procedure, not a lesser permit.** It is a streamlined process for
classes of straightforward, low-impact applications, and its features (per the departmental
*Guide to Victoria's Planning System*, chapter 3.8.13, opened 15 September 2026) are:

- the responsible authority is expected to assess the application **within 10 business days** of
  receiving it — an expectation stated in that guide, not a guarantee, and not a substitute for
  reading the scheme;
- the **CEO of the council** is the responsible authority, not the council;
- the application is exempt from the notice requirements of s 52 of the PE Act and from certain
  decision-making considerations in ss 60 and 84B;
- it is assessed only against the specific decision guidelines in the scheme;
- review rights on refusal are narrower — the applicant only.

**Which classes qualify is set by the scheme, not by this file.** There are state VicSmart
classes, applying in every scheme, and local classes that differ municipality to municipality.
An application is only a VicSmart application if **every** permit trigger for it is a VicSmart
class, nothing permitted would breach a registered restrictive covenant, and any required
referral has already been dealt with by the referral authority in writing. Miss one limb and the
regular process applies — which is the VicSmart equivalent of the all-or-nothing quality the NSW
file describes for a CDC, and it bites the same way: late.

### Metropolitan Planning Levy

For a permit application in metropolitan Melbourne where the estimated cost of development
exceeds a threshold amount, a **Metropolitan Planning Levy** must be paid to the State Revenue
Office **before** the application is made, and the SRO levy certificate must be lodged with the
application. An application that does not comply is void — not deficient, void. The threshold is
indexed annually and is published by the SRO; **no figure is recorded here**, because a levy
threshold copied into a knowledge file drifts out of step with the source that owns it. Read it
at sro.vic.gov.au at the time of application.

## The building permit

The building permit is a separate instrument obtained from a **relevant building surveyor**.
Under s 17 of the Building Act the application may be made to the **municipal building surveyor**
or to a **private building surveyor** appointed under Part 6 — the choice is the owner's, and it
is a real choice with programme consequences, unlike the planning permit where the responsible
authority is fixed by the scheme.

Before issuing, the relevant building surveyor must be satisfied (s 24(1)) that the work and the
permit comply with the Act, the regulations and any binding determination; that any required
consent of a reporting authority has been obtained; that any relevant planning permit or other
prescribed approval **has been obtained**; that the building permit **will be consistent** with
it; and that the building permit levy imposed under s 205G has been paid. The technical standard
the work is assessed against is the National Construction Code as called up by the Victorian
building regulations — which edition governs is a separate question, answered in
`codes/ncc/adoption-and-editions.md` and recorded per project, never assumed.

### The completion instruments

These are the close-out artefacts, and they belong on the programme as hold points with the
defects and handover items, not as paperwork after the fact:

- **Occupancy permit** — required in respect of building work, subject to the exceptions in
  s 21(2) of the Act. It is issued by the relevant building surveyor and, under s 46, is
  *evidence that the building or part to which it applies is suitable for occupation*. A person
  must not occupy in contravention of it (s 39).
- **Certificate of final inspection** — issued under s 38 where an occupancy permit is **not**
  required for the building work. The Act is explicit that a certificate of final inspection is
  **not** evidence that the work complies with the Act or the regulations, which is worth knowing
  before anyone treats it as a compliance certificate in a handover pack.

Which of the two applies is determined by the relevant building surveyor at permit stage — under
s 21 the surveyor decides whether an occupancy permit is required, for the whole or part of the
building. Ask at permit stage; discovering it at practical completion is too late to fix a
programme around it.

## The separate approvals people forget

Each of these is a **programme predecessor** with its own lead time and its own authority.
Showing them as parallel activities is how a Victorian fitout programme becomes optimistic.

- **Landlord consent.** Not statutory, and usually the predecessor to everything else — the
  application for a planning permit and the application for a building permit both proceed on
  the owner's authority.
- **Food premises registration** — *Food Act 1984* (Vic), Part VI. The registration authority is
  the **council of the municipal district** in which the premises is located (s 35(1)(a)). A food
  business must not operate from unregistered premises (s 35A). The trap is s 38B(1)(c): before
  registering, the registration authority **must inspect the premises** — an inspection that
  cannot happen until the fitout is substantially finished, and which sits between practical
  completion and trading.
- **Trade waste** — the **water corporation** for the area, not the council. Anywhere there is a
  grease arrestor or a commercial kitchen discharging to sewer. The instrument is named
  differently by different corporations (Yarra Valley Water issues a *trade waste agreement*;
  South East Water grants a *trade waste consent*), so establish which corporation serves the
  site and use its language in the correspondence.
- **Signage** — usually a planning question under the particular provisions rather than a
  separate regime, but it can also be a landlord and a base-building approval. Track it
  separately: it is routinely the last permit to land and the first thing the client notices.
- **Liquor** — **Liquor Control Victoria** regulates the supply of alcohol under the *Liquor
  Control Reform Act 1998* (Vic). It is a separate regime from the planning scheme with its own
  application, its own fees and its own timeframes, and a planning permit for a licensed use does
  not produce a licence.
- **Heritage** — two different things, commonly confused. A **Heritage Overlay** in the planning
  scheme is administered by the **council** through the planning permit. A place on the
  **Victorian Heritage Register** is state-listed and administered by **Heritage Victoria** under
  the *Heritage Act 2017* (Vic), which may require a **heritage permit or consent** in its own
  right. A VHR place can also carry an overlay. The Planning Property Report reports the VHR
  status; Heritage Victoria's own address search reports VHR, the Victorian Heritage Inventory
  and overlay status together.
- **Aboriginal cultural heritage** — the *Aboriginal Heritage Act 2006* (Vic) can require a
  **Cultural Heritage Management Plan** before a permit application, where a listed high-impact
  activity causing significant ground disturbance occurs in an area of cultural heritage
  sensitivity. Rarely engaged by an internal tenancy fitout, which is exactly why it is missed on
  the one job that breaks ground.

## What this means for a PM

- **Establish the zone and the overlays before the programme is built**, from the Planning
  Property Report and the scheme itself. Everything downstream is priced off that answer.
- **Ask early whether a planning permit is required at all.** A no is a real answer and shortens
  the programme; an assumed no discovered at building-permit stage does not, because s 24 stops
  the building permit until the planning permit exists.
- **Never programme the building permit as if VicSmart were guaranteed.** Confirm with the
  council that every trigger is a VicSmart class before relying on the shorter path, and carry
  the regular process as the position until they do.
- **Name the relevant building surveyor early**, and ask at permit stage whether the work will
  close out on an occupancy permit or a certificate of final inspection. They are different
  documents with different legal effect.
- **Track the ancillary approvals as their own programme lines**, with the council food
  inspection and the water corporation shown as predecessors to trading rather than as
  post-completion administration.
- **Use Victorian words.** Responsible authority, relevant building surveyor, planning scheme,
  planning permit, VicSmart, occupancy permit. NSW vocabulary in a Victorian document reads as
  unfamiliarity with the jurisdiction, the same way model-WHS vocabulary does — see `safety/vic.md`.

## What this file does not settle

Named rather than left to be discovered:

- **Which VicSmart classes apply to a given tenancy.** Set by the scheme, state and local, and
  not recorded here. Verified by reading the applicable scheme at
  planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au for that municipality.
- **The Metropolitan Planning Levy threshold and any fee.** Deliberately absent. Verified at
  sro.vic.gov.au and in the *Planning and Environment (Fees) Regulations 2016* at the time of
  application.
- **The statutory decision periods for a regular permit application**, and the prescribed period
  for deciding an occupancy permit. Not recorded here — the regular-process periods live in the
  PE Act and its regulations and the occupancy-permit period in the building regulations.
  Verified by opening those instruments for the job in hand.
- **The Building Regulations 2018 in detail.** Only the version listing on legislation.vic.gov.au
  was opened in this run (version 031, in force from 1 September 2026); the regulation text was
  not read. Verified by opening the consolidated regulations.
- **The Victorian Building Authority's own guidance.** vba.vic.gov.au returned HTTP 403 to
  automated fetch on 15 September 2026, so nothing in this file rests on it. Verified by a person
  opening the VBA site in a browser.
