# NSW approval pathways

Three possibilities for a tenancy fitout, in ascending order of pain:

| Pathway | When | Rough lead time |
|---|---|---|
| **Exempt development** | Minor internal work meeting the exempt criteria, no change of use, no structural change | None |
| **Complying Development Certificate (CDC)** | Meets the prescriptive standards; issued by a private certifier | Weeks |
| **Development Application (DA)** | Anything outside the CDC standards — change of use, external works, signage beyond limits | Months |

**A CDC is not a lesser DA. It is a different test.** It is prescriptive: meet every standard and it
must be issued; miss one and it cannot be, however reasonable the departure. That all-or-nothing
quality is why a CDC can fail late over something small.

The NSW Government exhibited reforms between **14 May and 24 June 2026** introducing a **CDC
variations pathway**, to allow minor departures from development standards to be assessed more
efficiently. Being finalised later in 2026. Worth watching — it directly addresses the failure mode
above.

## Statewide Community Participation Plan — in effect since 1 September 2026

A single statewide **Community Participation Plan (CPP)** now replaces more than 100 separate
council and agency community participation plans across NSW. Announced by the NSW Planning
Portal on 27 August 2026, and confirmed **in effect** — not upcoming — on the Portal on
10 September 2026: the item reads "replacing more than 100 separate plans currently in operation
across NSW with a new statewide Community Participation Plan (CPP) from 1 September", and that
date has passed.

A CPP governs how a consent authority notifies and consults the community on a development —
public exhibition periods, who is notified, how submissions are taken — not the approval pathway
itself (exempt, CDC or DA, above). Replacing a locally-set instrument with a single statewide one
is the kind of change that can shift an exhibition period or a notification trigger without
touching the pathway test itself.

## The separate approvals people forget

The building approval is not the only one, and the others have their own lead times:

- **Landlord consent** — usually a predecessor to everything else
- **Food premises approval** — council health, separate from the building approval, wherever there is
  a food offer
- **Trade waste** — the water authority, wherever there is a grease arrestor
- **Signage** — often its own consent, especially on a street frontage or in a heritage context
- **Liquor** — its own regime entirely, where relevant

Each of these is a programme predecessor. Showing them as parallel activities is how a programme
becomes optimistic.

## Design and Building Practitioners Act — and what replaced it

The **Design and Building Practitioners Act 2020** imposed registration, declaration and design
compliance obligations on practitioners doing "building work".

Two developments matter:

**Retail fitout was carved out.** Amendments removed **non-structural** commercial and retail fitout
from the definition of building work for mixed-use residential developments, provided the work is the
subject of a separate development consent. Commercial and retail fitout that **relates to a structural
component** of the mixed-use building remains caught.

That distinction is worth being precise about, because it turns on whether the work is structural,
not on whether the building is residential. A tenancy in a mixed-use tower doing purely non-structural
fitout under its own consent is outside; the same tenancy penetrating or altering structure is inside.

**The Building (Approvals and Practitioners) Act 2026 has passed**, consolidating the Design and
Building Practitioners Act 2020 and the Building and Development Certifiers Act 2018 into a single
Act. Commencement and transitional arrangements determine when it bites.

## What this means for a PM

- Establish the pathway **before** the programme is built. DA versus CDC is months.
- If CDC, treat every prescriptive standard as a hold point — one miss and the pathway changes.
- Ask early whether the work is structural. It decides the DBP position, and the answer is often
  assumed rather than determined.
- Track the ancillary approvals as separate programme lines with their own predecessors.
