# Permits and approvals in the United States

**Read this before advising on any approval.** Permitting is local. The same fitout can need a
different set of approvals in two neighbouring cities, and nothing here is a determination that a
permit is or is not required for a particular site. A person establishes the pathway with the
authority having jurisdiction (AHJ). These files tell a project manager what to ask and whom to ask.

**Provisional.** Written from general knowledge. What would verify it, for a given project: the AHJ's
own published requirements, recorded in a state or city file below.

## The approvals a commercial fitout usually meets

| Approval | Who issues it | PM trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Zoning / land-use approval | City or county planning department | Change of use, signage, exterior changes, parking |
| Building permit (with plan review) | City or county building department | Almost any alteration beyond cosmetic finishes |
| Trade permits — electrical, mechanical, plumbing, fire | Building department or separate trade divisions | Each trade's scope; often pulled by the licensed trade contractor |
| Fire department review | Fire marshal / fire prevention bureau | Sprinkler, fire alarm and life-safety changes |
| Health department approval | County or city health department | Food service, some medical and personal-services uses |
| Inspections | Building department and fire marshal | Rough-in, above-ceiling, final — each is a programme predecessor |
| Certificate of occupancy (CO), or temporary CO | Building official | Required before the tenant may occupy; a change of use usually needs a new CO |

Landlord approval sits beside all of these and is often the longest lead item on a tenant fitout.

## Built per state or city, on first use

When a project is registered, the PM records the AHJ in `project_facts`. Where no file exists at
`permitting/<state>.md` (or `permitting/<state>/<city>.md`), the PM commissions the knowledge
curator (with the owner's OK) to record that authority's permit types, plan-review process, typical
inspections and where its requirements are published — each with its source and `confidence:
provisional` until a person confirms it.
